# Changelog

All notable changes to this project will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- Select rows with Up, Down, PageUp, PageDown, Home and End, click a row, or scroll the tile under the pointer. Selection follows sessions and worker seats as their work changes. Role and status widths follow the painted rows while age units remain aligned through scrolling.

### Changed
- The accounts table drops optional columns before it wraps, keeps account and error lines in view, and marks cut summary titles with “…”.
- Reset shades follow time order: the soonest reset takes the nearest shade. On dark themes the shades go from yellow-green (soonest) to sea green (latest). On light themes a brighter violet means a sooner reset.
- Light reset times use new ramp ends: violet to deep violet in Default Light and violet to indigo in High Contrast Light. No reset shade now matches the darkest violet session color.
- The accounts table matches the accounts board: nine columns with lower-case headers, a separate `read` age, `enabled` and `in use` credit columns, and a green/red read dot. Session name colors stay apart from status, account and role colors. **Breaking:** user theme files must supply `session_hues` with exactly five colors under every variant; the old rainbow role keys are removed, and files without the list do not load.
- Reset times use their own green (dark) or violet-to-indigo (light) ramp, never error red or a low-quota ink; a failed reading keeps its red dot. Each top-level session takes its own color whatever the size of its subtree, and light themes use more saturated session colors. Theme files can set the ramp ends with `reset_nearest` and `reset_farthest`.
- Selected rows keep their text hues and adjust lightness for readability, reducing chroma only at the color gamut boundary; unselected rows retain their theme inks.
- Low weekly-quota values use the theme's readable ink on dark and light backgrounds.
- Role columns fit the roles visible in their tile group. Short tiles keep the branch column when it fits. Fitted column sets share widths and age slots after optional columns drop; the summary also omits columns without values. A shell still running after its timer expires reads `running` in the Busy ink.
- Tiles omit columns with no recorded values. Tiles with the same columns share their positions within a screen column; other tiles fit independently. Shell timers show the remaining time as digits, avoiding undersized pie glyphs. `thinking` and `running` draw in one green in every theme.
- Shell rows show `running` or their timer in the status column, with `shell` only in the type column. Status inks retain readable glyph coverage. Session-name sorting in tiles and the summary ignores case, with original spelling breaking ties.
- Footer role and account ink and status words remain readable on focused and unfocused tile backgrounds. Worker job labels skip worktree and director context; shortened labels omit whitespace before the ellipsis.
- Summary headers use `account` and `session`, and only sessions heading a hierarchy appear in that table; sessions without a hierarchy appear only in `unassigned`. Codex sessions use their own recorded names, activity and fast tier, and an open rollout alone no longer creates a worker row. Codex thread rows retain a missing account when none was recorded.
- Role words use the configured footer colors in tile titles and each accounts-table role. Subagents show their current transcript activity; nested work inherits its session account. Command names omit leading shell assignments.
- Account resets shade from the theme’s success color for the nearest reset to its error color for the furthest. Click any accounts header to sort that table; click again to reverse it.
- Plan-mode Claude reviewers show `review`. Live pool reviewers show their current review under their unit; idle reviewers and templates stay hidden. Claude-seat dispatches fold into their live session rows.
- Session detail readers start with small transcript tails and expand only to find the required values. Rollout head reads stop when the session metadata and first task are found. Later scans read only appended bytes and retain unfinished records.
- Production roles arrive before slow session detail reads. Hierarchy failures and unmatched sessions show a summary note; footer text colors meet 4.5:1 contrast on the active theme, including stale account labels.
- The summary can show stored account roles, weekly usage, resets and credits from a configured accounts command, refreshed in the background. Worker job names skip dispatch role preambles.
- Worker rows use their seat or role and first task instead of raw thread ids. Status shows the current tool activity or live verification step, including detached gates and Mac offloads; standard-tier accounts carry no mark. Fresh-seat and follow-up dispatches fold through their live launcher identity.
- Top-level sessions without a production role share one sortable `unassigned` tile per machine, retaining their own name colors and nested work.
- Click a tree or summary column header to sort its siblings or machine group; click again to reverse. The default `age ▼` keeps the existing order and name colors. Codex workers use hierarchy roles, then the folded dispatch’s session roster role or seat role token, or `worker`; every Codex worker name uses its parent session’s color.
- Codex thread rows show busy or idle from their rollout turn events. Their tier mark follows the tree account label.
- Worker launcher shells fold into their matching thread within the same tile. The worker row shows its short seat name and dispatched job; unmatched launchers remain visible.
- Role and account labels use the Claude Code footer colors from `~/.claude/config/statusline.conf`, reloaded on census scans when the file changes. Machine headings count top-level agents, and shell rows show `—` in the role column.

- Agent tiles draw one tree under a header row with `session`, `role`, `status`, `account` and `age` columns, plus `branch` and `directory` in the full view. Shell commands and subagents are tree lines; the list below the tree is gone. When two accounts of one program are running, the account column appears, and the header line and summary's `agent` column show account labels. Configure home-to-label pairs in the optional `[accounts]` table.

### Fixed
- Hierarchy refresh commands run on their own workers. Remote probes and settled local rows continue while a follow-up answer is pending; linked rows wait together, and checked session ids stay checked across catch-up answers.
- The first hierarchy wait is bounded to one second; Claude sessions and Codex thread carriers stay held when the answer is late, so a unit or worker cannot flash under the wrong owner. A late answer publishes during detail reads. Each new session missing from a hierarchy answer gets one extra check. Known links survive a PID change or a failed command.
- On startup, agents appear before their details are read. A refresh never drops details, a resumed session keeps its cell, and remote probes no longer wait for the local scan.
- A widened agents summary draws unbroken rows across column bands, including long directories.
- `appearance.mode = "auto"` selects the light or dark theme from the system before the first frame and follows changes while cargo-handler runs.

### Added
- Seat delivery commands now fold into their exact dispatched seat, including current and legacy launchers, review launchers, messenger sends and literal prompt-preparation wrappers. Ordinary shell work remains visible.
- Tile trees have a `type` column for shells and subagents. Subagent roles come from specialist metadata or their own work descriptions. Summary and tree ages use shared unit slots, numeric columns align to the right, and tiles in one screen column share their table positions.
- Showrunner and unassigned tiles start sorted by session name. Session hues omit management purple and yellow; account ink includes the glyph coverage margin, and light chrome uses a neutral accent distinct from account orange.

- `[tiles] view`, stepped under Tiles in the settings overlay, selects `summary` or `cells`; `cells` is the default; a saved `auto` loads as `cells`.
- The agents summary, when too long for its cell, takes the next cells of its column, pushing agent cells on, and gives them back once it fits; at the fit limit it is clipped as before. The summary also widens over the columns its depth opens, with `widen_summary` on by default.
- A shell row whose subtree ends in one `sleep` and nothing else is a timer: its `runs` reads `timer`, in the shell color, and its name a pie and the time left, such as `◔ 3:48`, in place of the Bash call's description. The pie fills by the quarter of the `sleep` gone -- `○`, `◔`, `◑`, `◕` -- and reads `●` at its deadline; the time left reads `m:ss` under an hour, `h:mm:ss` from an hour up, and `0:00` once past. The `sleep`'s arguments add up as GNU `sleep` reads them, each a number with an optional `s`, `m`, `h` or `d`; a `sleep` with an argument that does not read, such as `infinity`, leaves the row a command. The countdown moves with the ages, redrawn each second. The probe's children carry `timer` with the `sleep`'s `started` and `deadline`, still at schema version 3.
- Each Codex agent's service tier: the summary's `agent` column, the cell header's program and a Codex row's `runs` read `codex fast` for the fast tier it asked for and `codex --` for any other -- the last `thread_settings_applied` event in its rollout, else `-c service_tier=` on its command line, else the codex-pacer's history at the time the thread began -- and `codex` where no source names one. Each rollout is read only as far as it has grown since the last scan. The probe's rows and children carry `service_tier`, still at schema version 3.
- A top-level agent's cell holds every session it opened in tmux, sessions those opened included, as a tree hung from its header: one line to a session after `├─`, `└─` and `│` glyphs, giving its name in its own color, its status, branch, directory and age; in a cell too narrow for that line, the compressed view gives each session one line of its glyphs, name and status. The cell's table nests what each session runs under the row naming the session, one level down. A session no longer has a cell of its own. The agents that opened sessions have the first cells, then the rest, each oldest first.
- Sessions the Claude desktop app started are listed under a `Claude.app` row. The app's own Claude Code, run on a machine it reaches over ssh from `~/.claude/remote/ccd-cli/<version>`, counts as Claude Code; a session whose record names `claude-desktop` as its `entrypoint` is launched by the row standing for the process holding it, the app's server there, so the app's sessions stand as the tree of one `Claude.app` cell and as `session` rows in its table.
- Each agent's branch: the header's directory line reads `<branch> · <directory>`, and each session's tree line has a branch column. It is read from the `HEAD` of the repository the agent's directory sits in, following a worktree's or a submodule's `.git` file, without running git; a detached `HEAD` shows the start of its commit, and a directory outside a repository shows no branch. The probe's rows carry `branch`, still at schema version 3.
- `[appearance] transparent`, on by default and stepped under Appearance in the settings overlay: the grid paints nothing under its cells, the focused cell's tint included, so a transparent terminal window shows the desktop behind every cell, and the focused cell's border lights to mark focus. Off, the grid is painted solid in the theme's background while its cells are shown, and focus is the tint. The attract screen draws the same either way.
- First version, at `0.1.0-dev`: a terminal UI on `tui_pane`, reached as `cargo-handler` or `cargo handler`.
- A tile grid opening on the summary cell. `+` opens a cell, `-` closes an empty one, the arrows and Tab move the focus ring, and a click focuses the cell under it.
- The summary cell lists the top-level Claude Code and Codex agents on this machine and on each remote machine: pid, agent, name, status, age and directory, oldest first under a heading and a column-label row per machine. A Claude Code agent's age runs from its transcript's first line, so a session resumed or restarted keeps its age and its place. An agent started by another agent is left out, and so is a session another agent opened in tmux, found by the `tmux new-session` call in that agent's transcript, even when that agent's process has restarted since; so is a `codex app-server`, except the one the ChatGPT desktop app runs on macOS, listed as `ChatGPT`.
- Agent cells: each top-level agent gets a cell titled with its name. The cell's header gives the agent's pid, program, status, age, machine and directory, and for a launched session the agent that opened it; below it a table lists what the agent is running, each row's `via` -- how the agent holds it -- indented by level, then what it `runs`: its shell tool's commands (`shell`, running `command`), named by the Bash call's description; the Claude Code and Codex processes those start (`shell`); agent processes it started itself, such as a `codex mcp-server` (`direct`); a Codex app server that left its shell and names the agent in `CLAUDE_PID` (`detached`); a Codex app server's threads (`thread`); its subagents (`subagent`); and the sessions it opened (`session`). `runs` is drawn in the program's color, and a command in the shell color. The probe's JSON carries each row's `launched_by` and `children`, at schema version 3.
- A Codex row is named for the thread its `codex` started, matched in `~/.codex/state_<n>.sqlite` by directory and start time: the thread's name, else its first prompt, else the command line after `codex` or `pid <n>`.
- A `desktop` column before `directory` in the summary, and `· <desktop>` after the machine in each agent cell's header: the KDE virtual desktop the agent's terminal window is on, read at most every 5 seconds from one `KWin` script listing every window's pid, caption and desktops. The window is the one the agent's nearest terminal ancestor owns, narrowed by caption when that terminal owns several (Claude Code's session name behind its status glyph, or Codex's `<thread> | <directory>`); an agent tmux holds is found through its pane's session and the clients showing it. A window on every desktop says `all desktops`, and an agent with no matched window -- detached, remote without KDE, or off Linux -- shows `—`. The probe's rows carry `desktop`, still at schema version 3.
- `[machines] remote` in `config.toml`, edited under Machines in the settings overlay: ssh host names probed every 5 seconds with `ssh <host> cargo-handler probe`, over a shared control socket. A remote that fails keeps its heading with the reason: `unreachable`, `cargo-handler not installed`, a probe version mismatch, `timed out`, or the probe's exit status.
- A hidden `probe` subcommand that prints this machine's agents as one line of JSON.
- Theme roles `claude`, `codex`, `busy`, `shell`, `idle` and `unreachable` under `[variants.roles]`, set in all four built-in themes.
- Rainbow names: each agent cell takes the next color of a rainbow in cell order -- red, orange, yellow, green, cyan, blue, violet, then red again -- and draws its title in it, and the summary draws that agent's name in the same color, so a row and its cell pair up at a glance. A launched session takes a color of its own though the summary leaves it out. Inside a cell, the agent named after `launched by` and each session in the tree and the table are drawn in their own colors. The colors are theme roles `rainbow_red`, `rainbow_orange`, `rainbow_yellow`, `rainbow_green`, `rainbow_cyan`, `rainbow_blue` and `rainbow_violet`, set in all four built-in themes.
- An agent cell draws its full view only where it lies flat at the cell's width -- its header on one line, its branch and directory unbroken, a line to each session of its tree, and a table of what they run that cuts no name -- and the cell has the rows that view takes; otherwise it draws a compressed view: a line to each session of its tree -- its glyphs, name and status -- and a line to each row of its table -- what it `runs`, then its name, indented by level -- each line too long for the cell cut and ending in `…`. A top-level agent's cell leaves out its header and its branch and directory, as the summary lists the agent; a launched session's cell, which the summary leaves out, keeps its header, its branch and directory and its `launched by` line, each cut to one line. A cell asks the grid for the rows of the view it draws at its own width.
- The label color is kept for labels -- the table's column headers and `launched by` -- so nothing else reads as one: machine names, the `pid` before a number, the ` · ` between facts and notes such as `nothing running` or a heading's agent count are plain text, and a machine's heading in the summary is bold. The high-contrast themes' label colors, formerly the same as their text, are now their own.
- `[tiles] fill` decides how the grid's cells spread over its columns, stepped under Tiles in the settings overlay. `redistribute`, the default, keeps every column within one cell of the others, the taller ones first: twelve cells stand as three columns of four, thirteen as four, three, three and three, fourteen as four, four, three and three. `add_new` fills a column at a time, so the cell that opens a new column stands in it alone. The number of columns is the same either way.
- `[tiles] widen_summary`, on by default and stepped under Tiles in the settings overlay: the summary widens over the next columns until its widest line -- a machine's heading, or an agent's row with its directory written out in full -- fits or it reaches the right edge, keeping the height its own column gives it. The cells of a column it covers divide what is left below it, and a column whose cells would no longer fit there stops it. Off, the summary stays in its own column and cuts a long directory short.
- The attract screen from `tui_pane`: it comes on over the idle grid after a quiet spell, and on `a`. `r` draws one at random and `u` undoes that replacement.
- Attract favorites: `ctrl-s` saves the parameters on screen, `ctrl-o` opens the saved list, `m` shows one at random. They are kept in `favorites.toml`.
- The framework overlays: `s` settings (appearance, initial rows, remote machines, file paths, notices), ctrl-k keymap editor, `?` shortcuts.
- `config.toml`, `keymap.toml` and `themes/` under `<os config dir>/cargo-handler/`, with four built-in themes mirrored as TOML under `themes/`.
- On KDE Plasma the attract screen draws the windows behind the terminal once `assets/cargo-handler.desktop.in` is installed: `scripts/install-desktop-entry.sh --crate cargo-handler`, or the script given a binary named `cargo-handler`. `KWin` grants window capture by executable path, so without the entry the backdrop is the wallpaper.

### Changed
- Dark themes use a brighter error red so over-budget row counts and widths stay readable on focused and unfocused cells.
- `summary` always shows the summary alone, and `cells` uses cells whenever they meet their size floor.
