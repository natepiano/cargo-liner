## [Unreleased]

### Added
- The drifting attract screen now fills its cells with bars, and `t` swaps between those and the characters it drew before. Both read the same drifting numbers, so the key changes nothing but how they are drawn. Bars can be drawn part way into a cell, so a line moving a few cells a second travels rather than stepping, and turning with the arrows now carries every cell into the new direction instead of dealing the field afresh.
- `p` toggles how much of each command a cell spells out. Short is where the display now starts: every step of the process tree is still drawn, but each one is named and nothing more -- `cargo mend` in place of `cargo mend --manifest-path /var/folders/T/x/Cargo.toml --json`. `p` spells the lines out in full again, and the status line says `tree` while it does.
- A second attract screen on `2`, and the one the app now opens with: the whole window filled with characters in the colours of the desktop behind them, every line of them drifting on its own. The arrows point the way the lines drift, `<` and `>` change how fast, `v` lets the lines come apart or puts them back together, and `[` and `]` draw their speeds together or send them further apart. The speeds are dealt in lanes, so runs of neighbouring lines travel together with a slower and a faster run either side. `1` goes back to the moving band, which keeps whatever it was last steered to.
- The attract band now sets off left to right across the whole window with both of its edges fraying, so everything the animation does is on screen before a key is pressed. `v` cycles which edges fray -- both, leading, neither, trailing -- and `[` and `]` slow and speed how fast they change.
- The settings overlay now moves on the rebindable navigation keys rather than on hard-wired arrows, so `[navigation]` in `keymap.toml` says what walks the list and what steps a value. Enter and space still take the next value.
- Steer the attract screen's band while it is being shown: the arrow keys send it the way they point, `>` and `<` speed it up and slow it down (`.` and `,` do the same unshifted), and `+` and `-` widen and thin it, with `=` standing in for `+`. Holding a key moves it further per press the longer it is held. No letter is taken, so `f` still freezes, `s` still opens settings, and `a` still gives the grid back. The keys belong to the animation rather than to the app, so a second one added later binds the same keys to its own meanings -- they live under `[attract_moving_band]` in `keymap.toml` and in the keymap overlay under "Attract: Moving Band". Only live while the screen was asked for with `a`; left to come on by itself over an idle grid it takes no keys at all.
- Show the attract screen on demand with `a`, whether or not anything is running. It replaces the grid rather than drawing over it -- a strip of characters across a grid of borders and tables reads as neither one thing nor the other -- so what is on the screen is the animation and the status line, which says `attract` so a grid that has been put away is not read as an idle one. The panes come back once the strip has faded the whole way out. A screen that only ever appears when there is nothing to build cannot be looked at on purpose.
- Show an attract screen while no cargo is running: the desktop behind the terminal window, captured with the window's own contents left out, drawn as a band of characters travelling across the whole terminal in the colours of whatever is underneath them. Fades out as soon as something starts. Follows the window when it is dragged: the capture covers the whole display, so only the offset into it is re-read each frame. macOS only, and only once Screen Recording is allowed for the terminal.
- Keep the head of a cell's chain when the block is squeezed to fewer rows than the chain has and there is no room for an elision. The foot of a hand-typed command is the shell it was typed into, which every command in a terminal has; the head is what says an editor or an agent was behind it. A driver's cell is the exception -- the foot there is the command itself, and the pid the rows below point at.
- Rows tied on start time now read by pid, so a cargo draws above the cargo it launched instead of under it.
- Draw a pid with no family in the ordinary text colour rather than the muted one, which is the colour of the column headers -- a pid written in the header's own colour disappears into it. The chain block above a cell's table follows the same rule, so its foot and the `parent` cell naming it are drawn alike.
- Keep family colours clear of the two the display already spends: the column headers, so a coloured pid never reads as an uncoloured one, and the error colour, so a pid never reads as something having gone wrong. Measured against the theme in force, not fixed at startup.
- Hold the whole display still with `f`, and let it go again with `f`. Nothing is folded in while it is frozen -- no scan, no fade, no cell in motion -- and the status line says `frozen` so a held screen is not read as an idle one. The scanners keep going and their output is thrown away, so letting go shows what is running then rather than walking through everything that happened in between.
- Carry a `parent` column beside `pid` in a command's own cell, naming the nearest ancestor the cell actually draws: the cargo above it, which is a row of the same table, or failing that the step of the chain block over the table the command was started from. Never the immediate parent -- that is the pty and shim the capture opened, and a pid the screen shows nowhere is one the eye cannot pair with anything. A pid with cargo running under it is drawn in a colour of its own, and every `parent` cell pointing at it is drawn in that same colour, so a `cargo nextest run` and the invocations its suite starts pair off without either number being read. A driver drawn at the foot of its own cell's chain carries the colour there, where its pid is written instead of in the table, and the colour travels to the summary on the `pid` column, which ties a cell to its row there. Colours are handed out against the families on screen so no two of them share one, and are held for as long as a family is in view -- a build finishing recolours nothing. The palette is the solarized accent set, whose one design property is legibility against a light ground and a dark one alike.
- Report what sccache is doing along the summary cell's top border: hit rate, the hits and misses behind it, and the disk the cache occupies against the ceiling it evicts at. Only when a server is already running -- reading the stats would otherwise start one -- and the fields drop out in order as the cell narrows.
- Show how far along a compiling invocation is, from cargo's own unit counter. The reading rules the working-directory heading over the command, in the summary and in the command's own cell alike, which costs the table no width.
- Capture cargo's output so progress can be read at all, through a shim: `cargo-tile install` moves each toolchain's real cargo aside, runs it under a pty, and mirrors the output to `/tmp/cargo-tile/`. `status` reports what stands in front of each toolchain and `uninstall` undoes it.
- Answer to `cargo tile` as well as `cargo-tile`.
- Report progress for runs with no terminal too, by asking cargo for the bar it would otherwise draw only for a tty.
- Say when a command is `blocked` on another cargo's build-directory lock, which from outside looks exactly like a build that has not reached its first unit.
- Withhold a command's own cell while nothing is running under it, for the subcommands named in `commands.hidden_when_idle` -- `port` to begin with. The summary line stays.
- List `commands.hidden_when_idle` in the settings overlay, and write the config file at startup when it does not already name every setting.
- Carry a `cpu` column. It is the whole command's share rather than the cargo process's own -- `top`'s scale, so a build across eight cores reads past 700% -- settled over a two-second window and held for a second at a time.
- Head a command's own cell with what launched it: the parent chain above the command, outermost first and one space deeper per level, each row a pid and a command line. The processes that only passed the command through are left out -- the capture shim and its pty, and the shells and login processes above them -- except whatever stands at the foot of the chain, which is what tells a command typed by hand from one an editor or an agent ran. A driver closing its own chain takes that place, so its shell goes. The block never takes more than half the cell.
- Draw a command named by `commands.hidden_when_idle` as the last step of its own cell's chain rather than as a row in its table. `cargo port` is open all day and compiles nothing, so its row said the same thing on every scan while costing the cell one of the invocations it was opened for.
- Write along the foot of every cell what its contents ask for against the size the cell was given -- `content rows: ## (##+##)  r/c: ##/##` -- the demand green while it fits and red once it does not, split into the block above the table and the table itself. The width the demand was measured at appears between the two only when it is not the width the cell was drawn at, which is the one way the counts can disagree without either being wrong.
- Report test progress, not just build progress. `cargo nextest run` counts its tests the way cargo counts units, and the working-directory heading names which count is on screen: `building`, then `testing`. Runs with no terminal report too -- nextest draws no bar there, so the count is read from its per-test lines instead.

### Fixed
- The attract screen finds iTerm2's windows again, so it draws the desktop of the display the terminal is on. `TERM_PROGRAM` names a bundle, `iTerm.app`, and the `.app` was being compared as part of the name -- matching nothing, which left the frontmost application's window standing in for the terminal's.
- The attract screen falls back to the nearest display, not the primary, when the terminal's window centre lands on no display at all.
- The moving-band attract screen draws the desktop across the whole of each cell rather than only through the character's ink, so the colours line up with the picture instead of scattering to whichever corner each glyph puts its ink in.
- The attract screen draws at the right scale on a Retina display. The terminal reports its text area in the display's pixels while everything it is measured against is in points, so every cell came out twice its size -- the screen covered the window's full width but died just under halfway down it, with the desktop itself showing through the rest.
- The attract screen no longer leaves its bottom row unpainted when the window stands the full height of the display.
- The attract screen draws the desktop behind its own window rather than behind a sibling terminal window of the same size. Titling both windows yourself stopped the old marker from taking, and size alone cannot tell two equal windows apart.
- The drifting attract screen no longer reads as grey bars laid over the desktop. Only the character in each cell was painted, so the rest of the cell stayed at the background and the display arrived through the ink alone -- an eighth of a cell at the narrowest bar, lining up into a rule at every cell boundary. The whole cell now carries the desktop's colour, with the character standing brighter in it.
- The attract screen no longer draws the desktop from behind another application's window. Under an emulator that hosts its sessions in a server process the terminal was found by whichever application stood in front, so a window of that application -- one the same size as the terminal's -- was taken for this one, and both the display captured and the offset drawn at followed it about.
- The grid no longer re-reads the whole capture directory several times a second. A run killed before it could clear its own registration left the file standing, and one of those was enough to have every log since the last reboot read on every scan -- 38,000 of them on this machine. A registration whose process is gone is cleared away instead.
- The attract screen now draws the desktop of the display the terminal is on rather than the primary one. Where a window stands and where a display stands were being read in two different coordinate spaces, so a window on any monitor but the primary matched no display at all and the primary was settled for.
- The attract screen no longer follows another application onto its display. Under an emulator that hosts its sessions in a server process -- iTerm2 does -- the window this app is drawn in was being taken to be whichever window was in front, so opening anything over the terminal captured that application's screen instead. The window is found by the number it was pinned at startup, whoever is in front. Windows standing on top of the terminal are left out of the capture too, since a window covering this one is not one it is drawn over.
- The attract band no longer leaves a band of empty grid down the window while both of its edges fray. Every offset across it now begins its lap somewhere else, so the run between where the strip ends and where it starts again falls in a different place on each of them. The two settings with an edge to track keep their single start.
- The attract screen now finishes handing the terminal over instead of turning around part way. A command that started and stopped inside the half-second the fade takes sent it back in over a grid that was opening cells and moving them about, and a run of short-lived commands left it hanging there. Work turning up now sends it the whole way off, and it comes back only once the grid has stood empty for a few seconds.
- The attract screen now takes its steering keys once it is what the display is showing, rather than only when it was asked for with `a`. The drifting text fills the window, so an arrow reaching the grid underneath moved a focus ring nobody could see -- which read as the arrows not working at all. `s`, `q`, `f` and `a` still mean what they always did.
- A cell no longer asks for rows it goes on to elide. The demand counted the whole process chain while the draw gives that block half the cell and then gives up whole levels until what is left fits, so a cell drawing twelve rows asked for forty-five and sat in a cell sized for the larger number with the difference blank. The demand now measures through the same fit the draw uses.
- A command too wide for its cell now wraps down the process chain instead of being cut at the edge. It breaks at whitespace, falling back to mid-word only where no whitespace will do, and every line it carries on to is set to the column the command started at rather than to its pid. The block still never takes more than half the cell -- it gives up a level of the chain rather than the rows the table is owed.
- Cells no longer exchange places when the order changes. A driver's cell sorts by the earliest work still under it, so the last of a queue finishing carried it past whatever started in between -- and the two cells were drawn travelling through each other to get there. The overtaken cell now closes and comes back in behind the one that passed it, and every cell after it does the same.
- `a` now puts the attract screen away over a grid with nothing on it. Dismissing handed the answer back to the roster, which reads an idle grid as a reason to show the strip -- so the key was overruled on the same frame and the screen could only be left by starting a build. Work arriving still re-arms it.
- Hold the render loop to a fixed schedule rather than restarting the interval from the top of each frame. The wait is granted late by a varying couple of milliseconds, and measured from the current frame that lateness became the next frame's starting point -- so the period alternated between what was asked for and several milliseconds past it, which is what the attract band was travelling against.
- Stop forcing a full-screen repaint every two seconds while the attract screen is up. Rewriting every cell inside one frame of an animation reads as a tear, and the strip already paints every cell it covers, so there was nothing of anyone else's left to write over. One fires as soon as the strip leaves.
- The attract band jumped once a second, and often drew the desktop behind a different window than the one it was in. Both were the same tie: two terminal windows of the same size cannot be told apart by size, and which of them won changed from one capture to the next. The window is now settled once, by title.
- The attract band no longer stutters once a second. Where the terminal window stands was asked from the render loop, and that question queues behind the display capture on the process's one window-server connection -- so every capture cost the animation two or three frames. It is asked from its own thread now.
- Bring the grid back when the attract screen finishes leaving. The repaint loop goes quiet the moment the band is gone, and the grid was still deciding on the previous frame's answer, so the panes could stay empty until something unrelated asked for a repaint.
- Let go of a frozen display without the attract band jumping forward. The loop asks for no frames at all while frozen, so the draw before the one that unfreezes was the full repaint on its timer -- seconds back -- and the band travelled the whole gap in a single step. It now stands still for as long as the display does.
- Order the cells by when the work in them started, the order every table on the screen already reads in. A cell was placed where there was room for it and kept that place for as long as it lived, so a command that opened its cell early stood above one that had started before it -- and a driver named by `commands.hidden_when_idle` stood above everything, because the cell was ordered by when the driver was started rather than by when anything began running under it. A `cargo port` open since the morning now sorts by the run inside it.
- Wait out the rest of a frame after drawing one rather than a whole fresh interval on top of it, which made the period the draw plus the interval and made it a different length every frame. Anything that moves without an event behind it -- the attract band, a grid settling -- ran slow and uneven.
- Say `blocked` for the checks rust-analyzer issues. Its `--quiet` leaves a waiting cargo printing nothing at all -- no lock line and no counter, since it has not started compiling -- so the capture was empty and the row read as idle. The shim now drops `--quiet` from a `--message-format=json` run with no terminal, which leaves stdout byte for byte what the caller parses.
- Report a nested cargo's wait on its own row. A cargo the enclosing run starts goes round the shim -- which refuses to capture one run twice -- so it had no reading of its own and its state column stayed blank, while the lock message it printed was filed against the command above it. Every row now reads the nearest capture at or above it.
- Read a process's parent from the kernel where sysinfo left it unset, so a cell's chain no longer stops at `/usr/bin/login` and reports a shell with nothing above it. macOS refuses the parent read against a process another user owns, and `login` -- which stands between a terminal and the shell in it -- is root's. Whether the chain stopped there was a race on whether a scan caught that `login` before it took root's privileges, which is why a command run from Zed showed its editor some of the time and not others.
- Report the state of an invocation the lead command is driving, not just the lead's own. A nested cargo -- `cargo doc` under a `cargo port` lint run, say -- waiting on the build-directory lock now says `blocked` on its own row; before, every row but the lead was left blank. A row with no capture of its own stays blank rather than borrowing the reading from the row above it.

### Changed
- The moving-text attract screen's fast and slow runs of lines now merge into one another instead of meeting at a hard edge. The speed change was concentrated into a narrow run of lines in the middle of each span, which read as a boundary between two blocks; it is spread across the whole span now.
- The moving-text attract screen starts a quarter slower than it did.
- The moving-text attract screen draws its bands at varying thicknesses rather than all one size, so the field reads as a field instead of a ruled grid.
- The attract screens ask for thirty frames a second rather than one at every poll. A frame of them is every cell of the window and the terminal parses the whole screen for each, while neither animation has motion enough to tell a hundred and twenty-five of them a second apart.
- The drifting attract screen's fast and slow runs of lines no longer stand where they were dealt: the pattern of speeds travels along the field at half a line a second, so a line drifts from a fast run into a slow one and back.
- The attract screen's fade now takes about twice as long to cross, so `a` reads as the screen changing hands rather than as a cut.
- The attract screen now arrives and leaves over bare panes. The grid keeps its frames and loses its contents for the length of the fade, and goes the rest of the way out as the band comes the rest of the way in -- so the band has a background to settle into instead of going dark over nothing, and no content is ever on screen under a band still crossing it.
- `initial_rows` now says how tall the grid grows in a single column before it starts arranging itself into a square, rather than how many rows a column fills before the next one opens. At three, the fourth cell turns a column of three into a two-by-two and the tenth turns a full three-by-three into three columns of four.
- Draw every reading on the working-directory heading and nowhere else. The `state` column used to grow a per-row bar wherever one directory had two commands reporting at once -- but nothing in a Rust build gets past the build-directory lock, so the second command is waiting rather than compiling and that case does not arise. The column now says one thing, `blocked`, and joins the table only when a row is waiting.
- Make Tab and Shift-Tab walk the tile grid. The status line advertised them all along, but the app registers one pane, so the step had nowhere to go and did nothing. They now step the focus ring cell by cell in the grid's own order, wrapping at either end, where the arrows read it as rows and columns.
- Fade a finished row into the cell it stands on, rather than switching it to grey and holding that shade until it vanishes. The heading and column labels travel with the least-faded row under them.
- Give a cell that has run out of room the space its neighbours are not using, from either side of it, instead of dividing every column evenly. A cell keeps what its contents need and hands over the rest, so a summary showing six rows in a column of eighty gives up the other seventy-odd rather than half its share; the only floor is the height a cell stops reading as a cell below. A column where everything fits still divides evenly. A cell keeps what it grew to after its rows go away -- the room comes back only when another cell encroaches, at which point the whole grid re-divides from what its cells are actually showing. Where the room does not go round, the focused cell is served first.
- Keep the summary's command line to what says something about the work. `--manifest-path` goes, naming in absolute form the directory already heading the row, and `--message-format` and `--color` go with it, being how the caller wanted the output rendered; between them they pushed the subcommand off the edge of a narrow cell. Everything else stands -- `-p`, `--bin`, `--all-targets` all name what is being built. Anything past a bare `--` belongs to another program and passes through untouched, and a command's own cell still shows the whole line.
- Order the working directories in a cell by when their work began rather than by name, and the rows under each of them the same way. The directory holding the build-directory lock started before whatever queued behind it, so it now heads them instead of falling wherever its path sorted; inside a directory the rows had kept the order they were first seen in, which showed a lint that had just queued above the run it was waiting on.
- Give the summary one row per command, and for a `commands.hidden_when_idle` driver the commands it drives instead of its own row. A driver compiles nothing and sits in a directory of its own, while what it drives says where the work is; every other command keeps what it started to its own cell, so a `cargo nextest run` whose suite runs a cargo per case no longer buries the summary under them.
- Gather the summary by working directory rather than by command. Every invocation running anywhere gets a row under the path it was run in, so a directory where one build holds the lock and others wait on it reads as a single block, whoever launched them.
- Count what a cell actually draws when working out what it asks for, wrapping included. An invocation counted as one row however far its command line wrapped, so a cell full of long `rustc` lines asked for a third of the rows it went on to lay out; the count now goes through the same table layout the draw does, at the width the cell will have. The summary also counted one row per command and left out the working-directory heading and gap each of them draws under, and a command's own cell counted the ancestry chain not at all while counting a driver's own row and heading even where it draws the driver as the foot of that chain instead.
- Hold the summary cell's title off the corner glyph, with a space ahead of the word.
- Wrap a command line too long for its column instead of cutting it off at the edge.
- Close a cell by having the grid come together over it, rather than trading the hole through every cell after it.
- Draw a cell crossing between columns in the columns as they stand partway through the move, rather than as they were before it.
- Keep a cell carried by a closing column from travelling up or down as that column is pushed off the edge.
- Give a heading's reading a tenth of a percent when the plan holds more than a hundred units, where one unit no longer moves the whole number and the reading would otherwise sit still for several units at a time.
- Rename the `sub` column to `runs`.
- Put `command` ahead of `compiler` and `runs` in a command's own cell, so the command line starts in the same column it does in the summary.
- Leave `compiler` and `runs` out of the summary, where a row stands for a whole command rather than for one invocation.
- Start the working-directory headings and the rows under them one space in from the cell border, rather than two and four.
- Reject arguments that are not a subcommand. 0.1.0 parsed no command line at all, so anything after the binary name was ignored.
- Head a command's own cell with the directory the command itself runs in. A test run drives cargo in a temporary directory per case; those sorted ahead of a home-relative path and pushed the run being watched below the fold of its own cell.

### Notes
- Installing the shim is always explicit, never done on startup. It stands in front of every cargo run on the machine and changes nothing about what cargo does, prints, or exits with. Query invocations, this workspace's own terminal UIs, and nested cargos pass straight through.
- A `--message-format=json` run is captured down the no-terminal path, which mirrors stderr alone, so the JSON on stdout reaches its caller byte for byte.
- The shim publishes itself as `CARGO`, which keeps the tools that honour that over the path -- `cargo-clippy`, `cargo-nextest` -- coming back through it.
- A run already going cannot be captured. Installing during a build is otherwise safe: a running cargo holds its binary open.
- `rustup update` replaces the shim with a fresh cargo. Running `cargo-tile install` again repairs it, and is safe to repeat.
- The shim is POSIX `sh` and takes either `script` implementation, both told to flush the log on every write.
- A run that reached no unit and waited on no lock deletes its own log as it ends, having recorded nothing the grid could read.
- Without the shim installed nothing breaks: the `state` column stays out and headings draw no rule.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-08-21

### Added
- Establish cargo-tile as the starting point for a new `tui_pane` application, tagged `app-template-v1`. The crate is a complete TUI with no application in it: framework globals, the settings / keymap / global-shortcuts overlays, live theming, a status line, restart in place, demand-driven rendering, and input on its own thread. See `crates/tui_pane/docs/as-built/app-template.md`.
- Edit key bindings from the keymap overlay: Enter on a row captures the next keypress, checks it against every binding in force, writes `keymap.toml`, and reloads. The `?` overlay hands off to the same editor with the selected row already open.
- Show the cargo invocations running on this machine, one row per invocation, grouped by the working directory they were started from and ordered by path. A process is classified as cargo by its argv rather than its process name, so a wrapper or a renamed binary is still attributed correctly, and a start-time tie between two candidates resolves toward the newer pid.
- Tile the pane into an animated grid of cells, one per running invocation. The layout is a pure function of the cell count, growing by greedy fill up to `initial_rows` squared and then toward the next square. A cell moving between columns is drawn as two placements -- the piece leaving the old column and the piece arriving in the new one -- so it reads as sliding rather than jumping, and transition progress is fixed-point so the animation is deterministic. A command finishing in the middle empties its cell and the grid closes up around it, with focus following its cell.
- Show the running cargo-tile version in the pane title.
- Own theme content in the app rather than the framework: the crate ships its own theme variants, and its grid draws on the shared pane border in the inactive shade regardless of focus.
